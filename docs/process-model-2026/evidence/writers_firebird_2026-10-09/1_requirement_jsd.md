# Joint Staffing Designator Recommendation

## Recommended Joint Staffing Designator
**FCB Interest**

**Rationale:**
The requirement for FIREBIRD Group 3 UAS (Increment 1) exhibits characteristics that exceed Service Information but do not necessarily require JCB or JROC level oversight at this stage. The system is designed for deployment from multiple Navy ship classes (CVN 68, CG 47, DDG 51, LHD 1) and must achieve 98% interoperability with DoD Command & Control networks via Link 16 (CDD Section 2, Section 4 KPP 1). This indicates a joint dependency on Navy infrastructure and joint C2 architectures. However, the validating authority is the Army (Force Application Functional Capabilities Board), and the Milestone Decision Authority is the Army Acquisition Executive (CDD Header). The requirement involves a specific materiel solution (112 systems) with a defined unit cost ($4.5M per system) (CDD Section 3, Section 6).

According to CJCSM 5123.01A, FCB Interest is appropriate when there is a capability gap affecting more than one armed force or joint dependencies, and the program is at an ACAT I, II, or III level (or equivalent). While the specific ACAT level is not explicitly stated in the source document, the unit cost of $4.5M per system for a set of 9 platforms (1 GCS + 8 AVs) suggests a significant acquisition. The total procurement cost for 112 systems would be approximately $504M ($4.5M * 112), which typically falls within ACAT II or III thresholds depending on the specific program structure and whether it is treated as a single system or a set of systems. Given the joint deployment requirements (Navy ships) and joint C2 integration (Link 16), FCB Interest is the lowest appropriate level to ensure joint visibility and coordination without imposing the higher burden of JCB or JROC oversight, which is reserved for more complex joint-wide capabilities or higher ACAT levels with broader joint implications.

## Assessment Against the Assignment Criteria

**a) Significant impact on Joint Force Design:**
*   **Assessment:** The system is designed to operate in contested maritime and littoral environments (CDD Section 1) and deploy from Navy ship classes (CDD Section 2). This implies a joint force design consideration where Army assets are integrated into Navy maritime operations. However, the primary mission is tactical reconnaissance and strike against light-armored forces, which is a specific Army capability gap (CDD Section 1). The impact on joint force design is moderate, as it adds a new sensor/killer asset to the maritime domain but does not fundamentally alter joint force structure.
*   **Evidence:** CDD Section 1 (Capability Discussion), CDD Section 2 (CONOPS Summary).

**b) Reliance on materiel or non-materiel solutions external to the validating organization:**
*   **Assessment:** The system relies on Navy ship classes (CVN 68, CG 47, DDG 51, LHD 1) for launch and recovery (CDD Section 2). It also requires interoperability with DoD Command & Control networks via Link 16 (CDD Section 4, KPP 1). These are external dependencies on Navy infrastructure and joint C2 systems. The validating organization is the Army (CDD Header), so these are external dependencies.
*   **Evidence:** CDD Section 2 (Launch Environments), CDD Section 4 (KPP 1).

**c) A unique DoW capability, or one Service providing what others depend on, or insufficient resiliency or redundancy:**
*   **Assessment:** The capability is described as addressing gaps in "Persistent Tactical Reconnaissance and Strike" (CDD Section 1). It is not explicitly stated as a unique DoW capability that no other service can provide. The requirement mentions "Current theater assets lack the necessary blend..." (CDD Section 1), implying a gap, but not necessarily a unique capability. The system is crewed by 4 enlisted personnel per shift (CDD Section 3), suggesting it is a manned system, not a unique autonomous capability. There is no explicit statement about insufficient resiliency or redundancy in the joint force, only that current assets lack the specific blend of endurance, zero-infrastructure deployment, and kinetic response.
*   **Evidence:** CDD Section 1 (Capability Discussion), CDD Section 3 (System Composition).

**d) Significant resource impact including the proposed ACAT level:**
*   **Assessment:** The target unit procurement cost is $4.5M per system (1 GCS + 8 AVs) (CDD Section 6). The total acquisition objective is 112 systems (CDD Section 3). The total procurement cost is estimated at $504M. This is a significant resource impact. The ACAT level is NOT PROVIDED IN SOURCE, but based on the cost and complexity, it is likely ACAT II or III. The sustainment cost cap is $1,200 per flight hour (CDD Section 6).
*   **Evidence:** CDD Section 3 (Acquisition Objective), CDD Section 6 (Program Affordability & Life-Cycle Cost).

**e) A previous JSD designation:**
*   **Assessment:** NOT PROVIDED IN SOURCE. The document does not mention any previous Joint Staffing Designator designation for this program or related increments.
*   **Evidence:** None.

## Recommended Lead FCB
**NOT PROVIDED IN SOURCE**

The source document does not specify a recommended Lead FCB. The validating authority is the Force Application Functional Capabilities Board (Army), and the Milestone Decision Authority is the Army Acquisition Executive (CDD Header). Typically, the Lead FCB would be the FCB of the lead service (Army) or a joint FCB if the program is jointly sponsored. Given the joint dependencies (Navy ships, Link 16), a joint FCB or the Army FCB with Navy participation would be appropriate. However, without explicit guidance or a previous decision, a specific FCB cannot be recommended.

**Evidence Needed:** A statement from the Joint Requirements Coordinator or the Army Acquisition Executive specifying the intended Lead FCB, or a previous JSD designation that assigned a Lead FCB.

## Joint Dependencies and Interoperability

1.  **Navy Shipboard Integration:** The system must be deployable from and recoverable on CVN 68, CG 47, DDG 51, and LHD 1 classes (CDD Section 2). This requires coordination with the Navy for shipboard spatial constraints, power, and launch/recovery mechanisms.
2.  **Link 16 Interoperability:** The system must achieve 98% successful electronic data exchanges with DoD Command & Control networks via Link 16 (CDD Section 4, KPP 1). This requires integration with joint C2 architectures and potentially allied networks (CDD Section 4, KPP 1 Objective).
3.  **Spectrum Management:** Transmitters must obtain spectrum certification in compliance with joint frequency management policies to operate within standard military S-band and Ku-band ranges (CDD Section 5). This requires coordination with the Joint Spectrum Center.
4.  **Weapons Safety Assurance:** The remote weapon release mechanism must incorporate three distinct physical/logical inhibits, requiring explicit human-in-the-loop authorization (CDD Section 5). This may require coordination with joint weapons safety authorities.
