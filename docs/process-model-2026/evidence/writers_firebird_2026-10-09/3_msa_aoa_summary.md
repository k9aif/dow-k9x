# Analysis of Alternatives Summary

## Alternatives Assessed

Based on the approved AoA Study Plan (derived from the MDD decision of record), the following alternatives were identified for evaluation against the validated requirements in the Capability Development Document (CDD):

1.  **Status Quo (Baseline):** Continued reliance on current theater assets. The CDD identifies these assets as lacking the necessary blend of prolonged endurance, zero-infrastructure deployment, and immediate kinetic response required to counter highly mobile, light-armored forces in contested maritime and littoral environments (CDD Section 1).
2.  **Alternative 1: Incremental Upgrade of Existing Platforms:** Modification of existing tactical reconnaissance or strike platforms to meet specific endurance, payload (150–350 lbs), and shipboard deployment requirements. This approach focuses on retrofitting existing systems for Link 16 interoperability and shipboard spatial constraints (AoA Study Plan, Section "Alternatives to Assess," Item 2).
3.  **Alternative 2: New Purpose-Built Unmanned Aerial System (UAS):** Development of a new UAS system specifically designed for the 1 Mobile Ground Control Station (GCS) + 8 Air Vehicles (AV) configuration, pneumatic catapult launch, and autonomous recovery. This alternative addresses the specific technology maturity requirements for MTBCF, data latency, and payload capacity (AoA Study Plan, Section "Alternatives to Assess," Item 3).
4.  **Alternative 3: Hybrid Manned-Unmanned Teaming (MUM-T) Solution:** A solution leveraging existing manned assets for command and control with unmanned effectors for strike. The study plan notes this alternative must demonstrate how the "crewed by 4 enlisted personnel per shift" requirement (CDD Section 3) is satisfied or if it constitutes a deviation requiring waiver (AoA Study Plan, Section "Alternatives to Assess," Item 4).
5.  **Alternative 4: Non-Materiel Solution (Procedural/Training):** Reliance on existing assets with enhanced training and procedural changes. The study plan indicates this alternative is likely to be rejected if it cannot meet the KPPs for MTBCF and payload, but it must be formally evaluated (AoA Study Plan, Section "Alternatives to Assess," Item 5).

## Measures of Effectiveness and Results

The AoA Study Plan defines the following Measures of Effectiveness (MOEs) and Measures of Performance (MOPs) to be used for evaluation. Specific results for each alternative are NOT PROVIDED IN SOURCE.

**Measures of Effectiveness (MOEs):**
*   **Mission Success Rate:** Percentage of missions successfully completed to locate, track, and destroy light-armored mobile forces in contested maritime/littoral environments (CDD Sections 1, 2).
*   **Response Time:** Time from detection to kinetic engagement, reflecting the "immediate kinetic response" requirement (CDD Section 1).
*   **Operational Availability:** Ability to maintain access/connectivity at a 95% operational availability rate over a 72-hour surge period (CDD Section 4, KPP 2).

**Measures of Performance (MOPs) - Thresholds (T) and Objectives (O):**
*   **KPP 1: Interoperability:**
    *   Threshold: 98% successful electronic data exchanges with DoD C2 networks via Link 16 (CDD Section 4).
    *   Objective: 100% seamless integration across joint Service architectures and allied networks (CDD Section 4).
*   **KPP 2: System Availability:**
    *   Threshold: 95% operational availability over 72-hour surge (CDD Section 4).
    *   Objective: >= 98% mission-ready availability under sustained field conditions (CDD Section 4).
*   **KPP 3: System Reliability:**
    *   Threshold: Mean Time Between Critical Failure (MTBCF) >= 150 hours (CDD Section 4).
    *   Objective: MTBCF >= 300 hours with automated failover systems (CDD Section 4).
*   **KPP 4: Timeliness (Data Latency):**
    *   Threshold: Sensor telemetry/target tracking latency < 3 seconds (CDD Section 4).
    *   Objective: Full-motion video/payload coordinates < 1 second (CDD Section 4).
*   **Attribute 5: Payload Capacity:**
    *   Threshold: Minimum 150 lbs (optical sensors/laser designators) (CDD Section 4).
    *   Objective: Up to 350 lbs (SAR/micro-munitions) (CDD Section 4).
*   **Deployment Compatibility:**
    *   Fit within ISU-90 containers and CG 47/DDG 51 hangar bays (CDD Section 5).
    *   Launch from pneumatic catapult and CVN 68/CG 47/DDG 51/LHD 1 decks (CDD Section 2).
*   **Sustainment Cost:**
    *   Operating and support costs < $1,200 per flight hour (CDD Section 6).
*   **Procurement Cost:**
    *   Unit cost <= $4.5M per system (CDD Section 6).

## Cost and Capability Trades

The AoA Study Plan mandates a rigorous analysis of life-cycle costs against capability requirements. Specific trade-off data is NOT PROVIDED IN SOURCE.

*   **Cost Constraint:** The CDD establishes a target unit procurement cost not to exceed $4.5M per system (1 GCS + 8 AVs) in FY2026 dollars and a sustainment cost cap of $1,200 per flight hour over the 10-year service life (CDD Section 6).
*   **Capability Constraint:** The system must meet specific KPPs for interoperability, availability, reliability, timeliness, and payload capacity (CDD Section 4).
*   **Trade Analysis:** The study must evaluate how each alternative balances the cost of development and sustainment against the ability to meet the high-reliability (MTBCF >= 150 hours) and low-latency (< 3 seconds) requirements. For example, a "New Purpose-Built UAS" (Alternative 2) may offer higher capability alignment with the specific 1 GCS + 8 AV configuration but may carry higher development risk and cost compared to an "Incremental Upgrade" (Alternative 1), which may have lower development cost but potentially higher sustainment costs or lower capability ceilings.

*Analysis Needed: A quantitative trade-off matrix comparing the estimated life-cycle cost of each alternative against its projected capability scores (MOEs/MOPs) is required. This data is not present in the provided inputs.*

## Risk Comparison

The AoA Study Plan identifies the following risk categories to be assessed for each alternative. Specific risk ratings or mitigation strategies are NOT PROVIDED IN SOURCE.

*   **Technical Risk:** Maturity of technologies required to meet KPPs, particularly MTBCF (>= 150 hours) and data latency (< 3 seconds) (CDD Section 4).
*   **Integration Risk:** Risk of integrating with Navy ship classes (CVN 68, CG 47, DDG 51, LHD 1) and Link 16 networks (CDD Section 2, Section 4).
*   **Sustainment Risk:** Risk of meeting the $1,200 per flight hour sustainment cap (CDD Section 6).
*   **Operational Risk:** Risk of operating in contested maritime environments with the required level of autonomy and human-in-the-loop control (CDD Section 1, Section 5).

**Alternative-Specific Risk Considerations (from AoA Study Plan):**
*   **Status Quo:** Risk of mission failure in contested maritime environments due to capability gaps (CDD Section 1).
*   **Alternative 1 (Incremental Upgrade):** Feasibility risk of retrofitting for Link 16 interoperability and shipboard spatial constraints.
*   **Alternative 2 (New Purpose-Built UAS):** Risk of developing new shipboard integration for multiple Navy ship classes.
*   **Alternative 3 (MUM-T):** Risk of deviation from the "crewed by 4 enlisted personnel per shift" requirement, potentially requiring a waiver.
*   **Alternative 4 (Non-Materiel):** High risk of failing to meet KPPs for MTBCF and payload, likely leading to rejection.
