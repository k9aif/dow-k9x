Identify Deliverables

How to Identify Deliverables
Where to Look for Deliverables in RFPs/SOWs
* Look for phrases like "contractor shall provide," "deliverable items," or "required outputs"
* Note any mentions of reports, documentation, or data packages
* Identify statements about hardware delivery, software releases, or service provision
* Analyze descriptions of work products, artifacts, or end items
* Look for words indicating delivery: "submitted," "provided," "delivered," "furnished"
* Note acceptance criteria, quality standards, or delivery schedules
* Deliverables often appear in contract data requirements lists (CDRL)
* If they mention "technical data package," this includes multiple deliverable components
* "As-built documentation" indicates configuration deliverables
* Contract requirements often specify deliverable formats, quantities, and delivery methods
* Technical specifications may reveal test reports or analysis deliverables
* Performance requirements suggest demonstration or validation deliverables
* Delivery schedules often correspond to critical deliverable milestones
* If "training materials" are mentioned, this represents educational deliverables
Key Indicators and Language Patterns
* "Contractor shall deliver..."
* "Required deliverables include..."
* "Submit," "provide," "furnish," "deliver"
* "Documentation package," "data package," "deliverable set"
* "Final report," "interim report," "status report"
* "Hardware delivery," "software release," "service provision"
* "As-built," "as-designed," "as-delivered"
* "Acceptance criteria," "delivery requirements," "submission format"

Instructions:
* Examine the provided text, and identify all tangible and intangible products, outputs, or work products that must be produced and delivered
* Examine the text multiple times to ensure that you have not missed any deliverables
* Be extremely verbose and comprehensive in your descriptions, providing citations to the original text
* Consider both explicit deliverable requirements and implicit work products that must be created

Mandatory Output Requirements:
Utilize the following format for each deliverable, with bullets for the description and citation, and an empty line between each deliverable:
Deliverable # : 1 complete sentence identifying the deliverable and its primary purpose
* Description: From the perspective of the project, generate a complete description of the deliverable including its content, format, acceptance criteria, delivery schedule, and intended use. Include any quality standards, review requirements, or approval processes associated with the deliverable. Be comprehensive in your description with a target of 100-250 words, with no penalty for word count.
* Citation: Include a citation back to source content to make it easy to understand why we believe this is a deliverable. If there are multiple documents or sections, then include the document or section name in the citation, along with the original text.
Expected Artifact Output:
#Deliverable Identification Report
Section 1: Executive Summary
* Summarize the deliverables, providing common themes and delivery timeline analysis
* Use between 500 to 750 words in the summary
* Each paragraph must have a minimum of 5 to 6 sentences
* Be sure to provide the total number of identified deliverables and double check the number at the end
Section 2: Identified Deliverables
Section 3: Deliverable Analysis Matrix
* Organize deliverables by delivery timeline (early, mid, late, ongoing)
* Categorize by type (documentation, hardware, software, services)
* Identify dependencies and prerequisite deliverables
* Note acceptance criteria and quality standards
* Highlight resource requirements and delivery risks
Section 4: Contract Data Requirements List (CDRL) Mapping
* Map deliverables to potential CDRL items
* Identify delivery schedules and frequencies
* Note format and distribution requirements

Quality Assurance Standards:
DO NOT MAKE UP FACTS EVER. If you don't know the answer, state so. Structure your findings to directly inform:
* Contract planning based on deliverable requirements and schedules
* Resource planning considering deliverable development effort
* Risk assessment from deliverable dependencies and acceptance criteria
* Quality planning aligned with deliverable standards and review requirements
* Schedule management reflecting deliverable delivery commitments and dependencies
