Identify Objectives

How to Identify Objectives
Where to Look for Objectives in RFPs/SOWs
* Look for phrases like "project goals," "mission objectives," or "desired outcomes"
* Note any mentions of performance targets, success metrics, or achievement criteria
* Identify statements about intended benefits, improvements, or capabilities
* Analyze descriptions of end states, vision statements, or success definitions
* Look for words indicating intent: "achieve," "accomplish," "deliver," "improve"
* Note quantified targets, percentage improvements, or measurable outcomes
* Objectives often appear in purpose statements and executive summaries
* If they mention "increase efficiency by 30%," this is a performance objective
* "Meet mission requirements" indicates operational objectives
* Contract requirements often specify performance objectives and success criteria
* Technical specifications may reveal capability objectives and performance targets
* Evaluation criteria suggest prioritized objectives and weighted outcomes
* Success factors often correspond to critical project objectives
* If "cost reduction" is emphasized, this represents financial objectives
Key Indicators and Language Patterns
* "The objective is to..."
* "Project goals include..."
* "Intended outcomes are..."
* "Success will be measured by..."
* "Achieve," "accomplish," "deliver," "attain"
* "Improve," "increase," "reduce," "enhance"
* "Target," "goal," "metric," "benchmark"
* "Performance criteria," "success factors," "key results"

Instructions:
* Examine the provided text, and identify all specific goals, targets, outcomes, or achievements that the project is intended to accomplish
* Examine the text multiple times to ensure that you have not missed any objectives
* Be extremely verbose and comprehensive in your descriptions, providing citations to the original text
* Consider both explicit objective statements and implicit goals derived from requirements or success criteria

Mandatory Output Requirements:
Utilize the following format for each objective, with bullets for the description and citation, and an empty line between each objective:
Objective # : 1 complete sentence identifying the objective and its intended outcome
* Description: From the perspective of the project, generate a complete description of the objective including its purpose, success criteria, measurability, timeline, and expected benefits. Include any performance targets, metrics, or benchmarks associated with the objective. Be comprehensive in your description with a target of 100-250 words, with no penalty for word count.
* Citation: Include a citation back to source content to make it easy to understand why we believe this is an objective. If there are multiple documents or sections, then include the document or section name in the citation, along with the original text.
Expected Artifact Output:
#Objective Identification Report
Section 1: Executive Summary
* Summarize the objectives, providing common themes and strategic alignment analysis
* Use between 500 to 750 words in the summary
* Each paragraph must have a minimum of 5 to 6 sentences
* Be sure to provide the total number of identified objectives and double check the number at the end
Section 2: Identified Objectives
Section 3: Objective Analysis Matrix
* Organize objectives by priority level (critical, important, supporting)
* Categorize by type (performance, cost, schedule, quality, strategic)
* Identify measurability and success criteria
* Note dependencies and enabling requirements
* Highlight alignment with mission and stakeholder needs
Section 4: Objective Traceability Matrix
* Map objectives to requirements and success criteria
* Identify measurement methods and metrics
* Note responsible parties and accountability
* Highlight interdependencies and conflicts
Section 5: Glossary of Terms

Quality Assurance Standards:
DO NOT MAKE UP FACTS EVER. If you don't know the answer, state so. Structure your findings to directly inform:
* Success measurement planning based on objective criteria and metrics
* Priority setting considering objective criticality and alignment
* Resource allocation reflecting objective achievement requirements
* Risk assessment from objective interdependencies and constraints
* Performance management aligned with objective targets and timelines
