Identify Milestones

How to Identify Milestones
Where to Look for Milestones in RFPs/SOWs
* Look for phrases like "key deliverables," "critical decision points," or "major milestones"
* Note any mentions of reviews, approvals, or gate criteria
* Identify statements about phase transitions or lifecycle events
* Analyze descriptions of scheduled events, deadlines, or completion dates
* Look for words indicating completion: "delivered," "completed," "achieved," "demonstrated"
* Note decision authorities, approval processes, or go/no-go criteria
* Milestones often appear in project schedules and work breakdown structures
* If they mention "Milestone A, B, or C," these are formal DAB decision points
* "Critical Design Review" indicates a major technical milestone
* Contract requirements often specify delivery milestones and acceptance criteria
* Technical specifications may reveal test completion or verification milestones
* Performance requirements suggest demonstration or validation milestones
* Heavily weighted deliverables often correspond to critical milestones
* If "Initial Operating Capability" is mentioned, this represents a major operational milestone
Key Indicators and Language Patterns
* "Upon completion of..."
* "Milestone will be achieved when..."
* "Delivery of..."
* "Review and approval..."
* "Decision point," "gate review," "phase transition"
* "Demonstrate," "validate," "verify," "certify"
* "Initial," "final," "interim" (delivery or capability)
* "Go/no-go decision," "exit criteria," "entrance criteria"

Instructions:
* Examine the provided text, and identify all significant events, decision points, deliverables, or achievement markers that represent critical progress checkpoints
* Examine the text multiple times to ensure that you have not missed any milestones
* Be extremely verbose and comprehensive in your descriptions, providing citations to the original text
* Consider both explicit milestone statements and implicit deadline or completion requirements

Mandatory Output Requirements:
Utilize the following format for each milestone, with bullets for the description and citation, and an empty line between each milestone:
Milestone # : 1 complete sentence identifying the milestone and its significance
* Description: From the perspective of the project, generate a complete description of the milestone including its purpose, success criteria, dependencies, and impact on project progression. Include any approval authorities, deliverables, or decision points associated with the milestone. Be comprehensive in your description with a target of 100-250 words, with no penalty for word count.
* Citation: Include a citation back to source content to make it easy to understand why we believe this is a milestone. If there are multiple documents or sections, then include the document or section name in the citation, along with the original text.

Quality Assurance Standards:
DO NOT MAKE UP FACTS EVER. If you don't know the answer, state so. Structure your findings to directly inform:
* Project scheduling based on milestone dependencies and critical path
* Resource planning considering milestone resource requirements
* Risk assessment from milestone interdependencies and approval requirements
* Success measurement aligned with milestone achievement criteria
* Decision-making authority mapping for milestone approvals and gate reviews
