Integrated Business Systems (IBS) – Military Sealift Command (MSC) Dossier

1. Program Description
The U.S. Navy's Military Sealift Command (MSC), under the Business Systems Branch (Code N611), requires expertise and services for the Integrated Business Systems (IBS). This initiative supports the MSC’s globally distributed personnel both afloat and ashore by ensuring business systems are fully operational, integrated, and enhanced when needed. The systems are software-intensive, intended to sustain and optimize MSC’s logistics, strategic sealift, and special mission operations globally.
MSC’s mission is clearly stated on their official site: “MSC exists to support the joint warfighter across the full spectrum of military operations. MSC delivers agile logistics, strategic sealift, as well as specialized missions anywhere in the world, under any conditions, 24/7, 365 days a year.”  


2. Scope of Requirements
The contractor is tasked with full lifecycle support for MSC’s Defense Business Systems, specifically:
a. Systems Development and Implementation: New requirements for the MSC-AIS systems.
b. Sustainment Activities:
- Software development
- Deployment and release management
- Infrastructure support
- Break-fix support
- Help desk operations
- Training for users and technical staff
c.Relevant Functional Areas:
- Defense Business System support
- Automated Information System (MSC-AIS) management
 Professional services for IT and Business needs

Military Sealift Command (MSC) Profile
1. Who is the U.S. Navy Military Sealift Command (MSC)
The Military Sealift Command (MSC) is a major command within the United States Navy. It manages the replenishment and resupply of U.S. Navy ships at sea, strategic sealift of military cargo, and specialized missions like undersea surveillance and oceanographic surveys.
MSC operates a fleet of over 125 civilian-crewed ships and works hand-in-hand with the operational Navy and broader Department of Defense (DoD) partners.

2. Are they a vital component of another government entity
Yes. MSC is a vital subordinate command of the U.S. Navy, falling under the United States Fleet Forces Command and the Department of Defense (DoD) umbrella.
* It acts as the primary logistics and transportation command for the Navy and broader DoD during peace and wartime.
* Its role is critical to U.S. Navy global mobility, enabling persistent, forward-deployed naval presence.

3. When were they founded
MSC was formally established on July 9, 1949, following World War II.
* Initially named the Military Sea Transportation Service (MSTS).
* It was renamed Military Sealift Command in 1970 to better reflect its evolving and expanding missions.

4. What is their primary mission and who do they serve or support
Primary Mission (from MSC official site):
"To support the joint warfighter across the full spectrum of military operations by delivering agile logistics, strategic sealift, as well as specialized missions anywhere in the world, under any conditions, 24/7, 365 days a year."
Who they serve/support:
* U.S. Navy (primary)
* Other branches of the U.S. Military (Army, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard)
* Department of Defense (logistics and transport operations)
* Allied and coalition forces during joint operations
* Humanitarian assistance and disaster relief missions

5. Who are their fundamental customers
MSC’s core customers include:
* U.S. Navy Fleet (Carrier Strike Groups, Expeditionary Strike Groups, etc.)
* U.S. Transportation Command (USTRANSCOM)
* Department of Defense Combatant Commands (such as CENTCOM, INDOPACOM)
* Other U.S. and allied government agencies requiring maritime transport services

8. How are they aligned and broken into directorates or sub-organizations
MSC is divided into functional and regional directorates aligned under a unified command structure:
* N1: Manpower and Personnel
* N3/N5: Operations and Plans
* N4: Logistics

Other units include strategic sealift program offices, combat logistics program offices, and specialized mission program offices.

9. How does this relate to the MSC IBS contract
The MSC IBS (Integrated Business Systems) contract directly ties into MSC’s N6 Directorate — particularly the N611 Business Systems Branch.
Key alignments:
* N611 is responsible for:
o Managing MSC’s core business software systems.
o Ensuring compatibility and integration with Navy Enterprise Defense Business Systems.
o Overseeing the life-cycle support (development, deployment, sustainment) of these systems.
* IBS Contractor Responsibilities include:
o Supporting globally deployed ashore/afloat personnel systems.
o Enhancing and modernizing MSC’s business activities via software and IT professional services.
o Critical to MSC’s global logistics, transport operations, and command-and-control infrastructure.
Thus, the MSC IBS contract is a major enabler ensuring that MSC’s logistical and operational support capabilities remain uninterrupted and modernized across their entire worldwide AOR (Area of Responsibility).

 Summary
The Military Sealift Command is a cornerstone of U.S. naval power projection, linking forces and supplies globally. Through a globally dispersed network and a robust business systems backend (enabled by programs like IBS), MSC ensures America’s Navy remains ready, mobile, and sustainable anywhere and anytime. The IBS contract is not just an IT contract—it underpins the digital foundation of MSC’s operational effectiveness.

 Major Programs
MSC's operations are structured around several key programs, each designated by a Program Manager (PM) code:
* PM1: Fleet Replenishment Oiler Program – Provides fuel and supplies to Navy ships at sea.
* PM2: Special Mission Program – Supports missions such as oceanographic surveys and missile tracking.
* PM3: Prepositioning Program – Maintains ships preloaded with equipment and supplies for rapid deployment.
* PM4: Service Support Program – Offers towing, salvage, and rescue services.
* PM5: Sealift Program – Handles the transportation of military cargo and equipment.
* PM6: Fleet Ordnance and Dry Cargo Program – Delivers ammunition and dry cargo to naval forces.
* PM8: Expeditionary Fast Transport Program – Provides high-speed transport of troops and equipment.
* PM9: Adaptive Force Packages Program – Supports specialized missions with tailored capabilities.
Each program is managed by a dedicated office responsible for the operational readiness and logistical support of its respective fleet segment.
